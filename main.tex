\title{SAT-Based Approach for\\$L(h,k)$-Labeling of General Graphs\\[0.5em]
{\large Technical research report: methods and experiments}}
\author{}
\date{October 18, 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
We develop a Boolean satisfiability (SAT) approach to minimizing the span of
$L(h,k)$-labelings of simple undirected graphs, with $h\geq k\geq1$.
Order encoding represents labels using threshold variables. A bounded
search uses a greedy labeling as an upper bound and distinguishes feasible
solutions from proven optima. The encoding also handles disconnected graphs
and does not rely on a particular graph family. An assignment integer linear
programming (ILP) formulation serves as an exact baseline. We establish
encoding equivalence, specify the conditions for symmetry breaking, and
provide a procedure for storing and independently validating labelings.
A completed confirmation on 117 instances across trees, grids and two random
graph models uses three repetitions and a 30-second external limit.
SAT and ILP establish optimality in all three runs on
\ProgressSATStable{} and \ProgressILPStable{} instances, respectively.
Feasible upper bounds and recorded lower bounds favor different backends
on some unresolved instances. Supplementary mathematical bounds,
color-class constructions and proof-producing SAT search independently
certify the optimal span of all 117 instances. The certificate pipeline
combines weighted independent-set capacities with a color-rank-residual
formulation and replays 11 clausal UNSAT proofs. This post-hoc
certification does not change timed solver coverage.
Supplementary tree, symmetry and fixed-budget genetic-heuristic studies are
retained with their own protocols. The GA attains independently certified
optima on more sampled tasks than an equal-evaluation random-order control,
with the descriptive difference concentrated in ER $L(3,2)$ cases. These
results establish correctness and describe computational limits on the
evaluated graphs, without claiming universal SAT or GA superiority.
The timed solver does not reuse learned clauses across span trials.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} $L(h,k)$-labeling, SAT, order encoding,
span minimization, ILP baseline, reproducible experiments.
