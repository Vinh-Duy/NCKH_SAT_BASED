\title{SAT-Based Approach for\\$L(h,k)$-Labeling of General Graphs\\[0.5em]
{\large Research manuscript: methods and experiments}}
\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
We develop a Boolean satisfiability (SAT) approach to minimizing the span of
$L(h,k)$-labelings of simple undirected graphs, with $h\geq k\geq1$.
Order encoding represents labels using threshold variables. A bounded
search uses a greedy labeling as an upper bound and distinguishes feasible
solutions from proven optima. The encoding also handles disconnected graphs
and does not rely on a particular graph family. An assignment integer linear
programming (ILP) formulation serves as an exact baseline. We establish
encoding equivalence, specify the conditions for symmetry breaking, and
provide a procedure for storing and independently validating labelings.
The current $L(3,2)$ study includes \TreeGraphCount{} random trees with
100--1600 vertices: both backends report the same optimal span on every
tree, and all \TreeObservationCount{} labelings have been revalidated.
The degree-based lower bound is attained on \TreeTightCount{} trees.
Archived $L(2,1)$ results on cycles and graph products provide supplementary
symmetry experiments. These results do not establish a performance advantage
on general graphs. A pilot study comprising 117 instances across several
families yields 79 instances solved to optimality by SAT and 97 by ILP
within 30 seconds per run; all 12 instances with exact reference formulas
agree with both backends. A confirmation experiment with three repetitions
on the same cohort yields \ConfirmSATStable/117 instances solved to optimality
in all three runs by SAT and \ConfirmILPStable/117 by ILP. The current solver
does not use incremental SAT or export independently checkable UNSAT certificates.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} $L(h,k)$-labeling, SAT, order encoding,
span minimization, ILP baseline, reproducible experiments.
