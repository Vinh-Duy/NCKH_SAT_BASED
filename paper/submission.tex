\begin{abstract}
We study an exact SAT approach to minimum-span $L(h,k)$-labeling of
simple undirected graphs with $h\geq k\geq1$. An order encoding and
bounded span search are proved correct and compared with assignment
integer linear programming (ILP). The original timed comparison covers
39 graphs crossed with three parameter pairs, giving 117
graph--parameter tasks and three operational repetitions per backend.
ILP has greater all-repetition optimality coverage, while feasible upper
bounds favor different backends on some tasks unresolved within the
30-second limit. A later, post-hoc proof package independently certifies
those same 117 tasks; its certificates do not change the timed outcomes.
A separate 69-graph cohort contains 207 graph--parameter tasks and 1,242
method-level rows for six methods. The parameter pairs are configurations,
not repeated measurements, and this finite graph cohort is not a random
sample of the entire graph space.
A fixed-budget genetic-algorithm baseline on the original cohort attains
a certified optimum in at least one of ten runs on 95 tasks, compared
with 85 for paired random-order multi-start. A separate $L(2,1)$ symmetry
study reports 494 jointly optimal pairs out of 496, with family-dependent
model-size and runtime effects.
Finally, an isolated Threshold encoding reduces clauses and literals in
the tested fixed-span cohort, but Phase 3E/3F show mixed solve-time
behavior and do not establish a practical speed advantage. Phase 3F
returns 42 valid SAT results and six parent timeouts across 48 scheduled
runs; no timeout is classified as UNSAT. The primary contribution is an
auditable exact/heuristic comparison, with Threshold retained as a
secondary exploratory result.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} $L(h,k)$-labeling, SAT, order encoding,
exact optimization, ILP, optimality certificates.
