\begin{abstract}
We study an exact Boolean satisfiability (SAT) approach to minimum-span
$L(h,k)$-labeling of simple undirected graphs, with $h\geq k\geq1$.
Threshold variables provide an order encoding, and a bounded span search
separates feasible labelings from proven optima. We establish encoding
equivalence and the search invariant, specify valid symmetry conditions,
and compare the approach with assignment integer linear programming (ILP).
A fixed cohort of 117 instances includes trees, grids, Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi
and Barab\'asi--Albert graphs with three parameter pairs. Under a 30-second
external limit and three repetitions, SAT and ILP establish optimality in
every repetition on \ProgressSATStable{} and \ProgressILPStable{} instances,
respectively. Validated feasible bounds and recorded lower bounds favor
different backends on some unresolved instances.
Separately, combinatorial certificates combine square-clique lower bounds
with explicit labelings. A color-class ordering construction closes
\ConstructionNewlyClosed{} additional instances beyond the earlier
clique analysis, bringing the combined evidence to \ConstructionClosed{}
closed cases and \ConstructionOpen{} open intervals. These post-hoc results
do not change timed solver coverage. The study establishes correctness and
documents computational limits within the evaluated cohort, without claiming
universal SAT superiority or a new formula for an entire graph family.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} $L(h,k)$-labeling, SAT, order encoding,
exact optimization, ILP, optimality certificates.
