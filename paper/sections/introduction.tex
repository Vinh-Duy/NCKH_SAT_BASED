\section{Introduction and research questions}
The $L(h,k)$-labeling problem models label-separation requirements at two
graph distances and originates in frequency assignment \cite{griggs,calamoneri}.
This study considers a SAT solver applicable to an input graph and a pair
$(h,k)$. Generality refers to the scope of the formulation: a finite benchmark
set neither represents all graphs nor guarantees efficient solution of every instance.

The focus is exact optimization and the strength of the evidence supporting
its results. We do not assume that SAT must outperform ILP on every input.
Four questions guide the study:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Do the encoding and span search preserve feasibility, bounds, and
optimality conclusions across graphs and parameter pairs $(h,k)$?
\item Where exact formulas or proven bounds apply, do solver results agree
with them, and which bounds are attained on the evaluated instances?
\item How do total computational cost, model size, and the ability to prove
optimality differ between SAT and assignment ILP as graph size and structure vary?
\item On families with justified symmetries, how do symmetry-breaking
constraints affect CNF size and solution time?
\end{enumerate}
The first question is addressed through proofs and tests against an exhaustive
oracle. Reference formulas with explicit applicability conditions are separated
from general bounds and final search bounds. The tree experiments and the
multi-family pilot provide initial evidence of agreement and the ability to
establish optimality within a time limit. The supplementary $L(2,1)$ experiments
partially address the fourth question. Symmetry experiments do not replace
SAT--ILP comparisons, and results on trees do not establish performance on all graphs.

\subsection{Contributions and scope of evidence}
The current study provides the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An $L(h,k)$ SAT pipeline, including a proof of feasibility equivalence
for the threshold encoding and a span-search invariant, together with an
assignment ILP formulation as an exact baseline.
\item An independent labeling-validation procedure, configuration and witness
records, and comparisons with formulas and bounds within their validity domains,
distinguishing established optima from feasible solutions at the time limit.
\item An evaluation of optimality coverage and computational cost on structured
and random instances, including cases where SAT or ILP does not establish
optimality, rather than timing comparisons restricted to easy instances.
\end{enumerate}
These are the contributions supported by the current manuscript. They do not
claim that order encoding is new or that SAT always outperforms ILP. Bounds
for structured graphs serve as validation references. Finite experiments do
not establish a new formula for an entire family or resolve an open conjecture;
such claims require separate proofs and comparison with prior work. The
related-work section identifies established techniques and distinguishes
between labeling variants.
