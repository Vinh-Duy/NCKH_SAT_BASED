\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\subsection{Interpretation of the results}
The four research questions have different types of answers.
For RQ1, the encoding equivalence and span-search invariant establish
correctness: a feasible labeling supplies an upper bound, and the search
reports optimality only when justified bounds meet. The implementation
checks decoded labelings independently; solver-only lower bounds still
rely on the backend's correctness because UNSAT and dual certificates
are not independently replayed.

For RQ2, the 12 applicable exact-reference instances agree with theory.
Combining solver intervals, theoretical bounds and new constructions
closes \ConstructionClosed{} of 117 instances, leaving
\ConstructionOpen{} open. Within the retained evidence,
\SupportIndependent{} closures have mathematical support independent
of solver bounds; the other \SupportSolverDependent{} depend on
recorded solver lower bounds. The \ConstructionCliqueCertified{} direct
clique-plus-labeling certificates are included in that total. The
color-order condition provides a sufficient construction and three
new matching witnesses, rather than a formula for all graphs in a family.

For RQ3, Gurobi proves optimality more consistently on the full cohort,
while CaDiCaL has lower median times on the common solved subset and
better feasible bounds on part of the initially unresolved subset.
Coverage, time and bound quality therefore describe distinct strengths.
For RQ4, the paired symmetry study shows smaller CNFs on the tested
families with added constraints, but the runtime effect varies. Cycles
have a median clause reduction of 12.58\% with a median time ratio near
one. The evidence supports descriptive, family-specific effects rather
than a universal speedup.

All 16 intervals remaining open after postprocessing concern ER graphs
with $p=0.15$; all five certificate closures concern $p=0.30$. This is a
pattern in the present cohort and certification method, not a general
ranking of ER difficulty. The timed comparison also contains difficult
dense instances, as Table~\ref{tab:family-runtime} shows. The matching
construction applies to any graph square, but restricts color classes
to pairs and singletons. On sparse squares, larger independent sets may
be needed to approach the clique bound. General square-coloring followed
by compatible class ordering is a possible extension, with no guarantee
of attaining that bound. The tested random trees are solved within their
budgets; this does not imply that arbitrary $L(3,2)$ tree instances are easy.

\subsection{Limitations}
The primary comparison is limited to 20--60 vertices, three parameter
pairs and three graph seeds per randomized configuration. Results depend
on the chosen formulations, backends and time budget. The construction
was designed after examining unresolved cases; testing it on the whole
cohort records failures as well as successes but is not a held-out
assessment. The symmetry experiment has only one measurement per
configuration and cannot establish statistical significance. Neither
experiment tests direct versus order encoding, incremental SAT, a
specialized tree algorithm or a GA baseline. These limits qualify performance conclusions without weakening
checked labeling and clique certificates.

\subsection{Conclusion}
The study provides an exact general-graph formulation and a reproducible
account of where the evaluated methods prove optimality, retain useful
bounds or remain unresolved. Independent UNSAT traces, a controlled encoding ablation and solver reuse
across spans would strengthen the evidence. Coloring-based certificate
search may supply further optimal witnesses, but is not part of the
reported experiment.
