\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\subsection{Interpretation of the results}
The five research questions have different types of answers.
For RQ1, the encoding equivalence and span-search invariant establish
correctness: a feasible labeling supplies an upper bound, and the search
reports optimality only when justified bounds meet. The implementation
checks decoded labelings independently. A supplementary proof stage now
replays the UNSAT evidence needed beyond the mathematical bounds.

For RQ2, the 12 applicable exact-reference instances agree with theory.
The earlier postprocessing closed 101 intervals and left 16 open.
Appendix~\ref{sec:proof-closure} closes all 16 and supplies independent
support for the entire cohort: 91 theory/clique certificates, 15
weighted-capacity certificates and 11 replayed UNSAT proofs. Ten upper
bounds improve. These disjoint categories total 117; none changes
original run statuses or timed solver coverage.

For RQ3, Gurobi meets the all-three-OPT criterion on 91/117 instances,
compared with 77/117 for CaDiCaL. Timing comparisons cover only their
74-instance intersection. CaDiCaL has lower medians in the tree, grid
and BA subsets, whereas Gurobi has lower medians in the ER $p=0.30$
subsets for $(1,1)$ and $(2,1)$. Each of those ER timing subsets has
only two instances. The pooled median does not establish a full-cohort
speed ranking. SAT supplies better feasible bounds on part of the
initially unresolved subset; coverage, conditional time and bound
quality are distinct measures.
For RQ4, the paired symmetry study shows smaller CNFs on the tested
families with added constraints, but the runtime effect varies. Cycles
have a median clause reduction of 12.58\% with a median time ratio near
one. The evidence supports descriptive, family-specific effects rather
than a universal speedup.

The expanded six-method benchmark contributes a separate finite
69-graph, 207-task comparison. ILP records more optimal outcomes than
the SAT variants, while GA and random-order methods provide validated
feasible solutions on many tasks without an optimality claim. These
status profiles do not establish a universal speed ordering, and the
69-graph cohort is not an independent random sample of all graphs.

For RQ5, the Threshold prototype reduces formula size in the tested
fixed-span models, but the measured solve-time direction is mixed and
does not establish practical solver superiority. The eight-graph Phase 3F
result is Decision D, not a basis for broad external-validity claims.

The supplementary GA obtains a certified optimum in at least one of ten
fixed-budget runs on 95/117 tasks, compared with 85/117 for equal-evaluation
random-order search. Its paired-median advantage is concentrated in ER
$L(3,2)$ cases. This is descriptive evidence for one adapted GA
configuration on the fixed cohort; it does not establish general
metaheuristic superiority or a runtime advantage over SAT/ILP.

The 16 formerly open intervals all concern ER graphs with $p=0.15$.
Their closure uses arbitrary square-independent color classes, weighted
capacity bounds and a color-rank-residual SAT encoding. These are targeted
post-hoc improvements and do not establish a general ranking of ER
instance difficulty or an encoding speed advantage. The tested random trees are solved within their
budgets; this does not imply that arbitrary $L(3,2)$ tree instances are easy.

\subsection{Limitations}
The primary comparison is limited to 20--60 vertices, three parameter
pairs and three graph seeds per randomized configuration. Results depend
on the chosen formulations, backends and time budget. Model size alone
does not explain the observed timing differences: this experiment does
not isolate constraint density, construction overhead and solver search.
The construction
was designed after examining unresolved cases. Its subsequent
fixed-protocol evaluation on 69 new graphs supplies separate evidence:
insertion order certifies 43/45 dense-ER instances but none of the
sparse-ER instances. DSATUR with spacing $h$ certifies more cases
overall, so the construction has no universal advantage. This study
uses one algorithmic seed and does not compare runtimes. The symmetry experiment has only one measurement per
configuration and cannot establish statistical significance. The GA
comparison uses one fixed hyperparameter configuration and one finite graph
cohort; its timer excludes shared constraint preprocessing and witness
validation, so it is not compared directly with SAT/ILP wall times. These
limits qualify performance conclusions without weakening checked labeling
and clique certificates.

The separate Phase 3A--3F investigation supports a narrower encoding
conclusion. Threshold reduces formula size in the tested fixed-span
cohorts, but solve-time direction varies, Phase 3F has only eight graphs,
and six runs are parent timeouts rather than UNSAT results. Fixed-span
SAT witnesses do not establish optimality, and $U=111$ remains
unclassified. IPASIR-UP telemetry was intrusive and incomplete; its
measurements are not treated as canonical evidence. These results do not
establish general solver superiority or external validity.

\subsection{Conclusion}
The primary contribution is an auditable exact/heuristic comparison:
feasible witnesses, optimality proofs, post-hoc certificates and censored
outcomes remain distinguishable across the separate benchmark
populations. The proof-closure package certifies the original 117 timed
graph--parameter tasks after the timed experiment; it does not certify
the separate 207-task six-method benchmark. The fixed-budget GA comparison
adds a feasible-solution baseline, and the construction study identifies
where its coloring-based certificates help and where they remain
insufficient. A matching-bound checker adds a proof format but closes no
further interval in the evaluated cohorts.

Threshold is a secondary exploratory contribution: its fixed-span
formula-size reductions are established only for the tested models, while
practical solver advantage remains unproven. Retain the Reference
encoding as the production/default path. Phase 3A--3F are closed for the
stated objectives; no Phase 3G is recommended. None of the finite graph
cohorts establishes universal algorithmic performance.
