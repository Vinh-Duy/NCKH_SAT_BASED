\section{Conclusion}
We presented a SAT implementation for minimum-span $L(h,k)$-labeling of
simple undirected graphs, including order-encoding equivalence, the
span-search bound invariant, and conditions for valid symmetry breaking.
Comparison with assignment ILP on 50 $L(3,2)$ trees yields the same optimal
span for every tree: 48 attain the degree bound and two require larger spans.
The multi-family pilot contains 117 instances, of which 79 are OPT for
CaDiCaL and 97 for Gurobi. The 78 instances solved to optimality by both
have matching values. All 12 instances with exact reference formulas agree
with both backends, and all 234 pilot witnesses are valid. These results
support an assessment of exact methods and their computational limits,
not a claim that SAT dominates across graph families.

The three-repetition confirmation on the same 117 instances records
\ConfirmSATOpt/351 OPT observations for SAT and \ConfirmILPOpt/351 for ILP.
Under the all-three-OPT criterion, the backends solve \ConfirmSATStable{}
and \ConfirmILPStable{} instances, respectively, with \ConfirmJointStable{}
in their intersection. Runtime statistics on this intersection must be
read alongside coverage and do not represent instances remaining FEASIBLE.
Equal stored intervals on the unresolved subset arise from shared initial
fallbacks after external timeout, so they do not establish comparable native
solver bound quality. Progress capture must be improved before that comparison.
Broader formula/bound comparisons, incremental SAT, UNSAT certificates,
and specialized algorithm baselines require separate implementation and
evaluation. Edge labeling and $L(h,1,1)$ are distinguished in the related
work and are not mixed with the present results.

Across the three methodological perspectives, theory supplies values or
bounds within stated domains, while SAT/ILP provides a means to find and
certify solutions on general inputs within a computational budget.
Common-span model size measures representation size, not runtime rank.
The adapted GA will be evaluated after the SAT--ILP comparison is consolidated,
using upper-bound quality and solution cost. There is currently no experimental
evidence establishing superiority or inferiority of SAT/ILP relative to GA.
