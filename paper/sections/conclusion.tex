\subsection{Conclusion}
We presented a SAT pipeline for minimum-span $L(h,k)$-labeling of simple
undirected graphs, with order-encoding equivalence, a span-search bound
invariant and conditions for valid symmetry breaking. The primary v2
comparison evaluates 117 fixed graph/parameter instances with three
repetitions and a 30-second limit per backend. SAT and assignment ILP
establish optimality in all three runs on \ProgressSATStable{} and
\ProgressILPStable{} instances, respectively. Runtime summaries are
conditional on the 74 instances solved optimally in every repetition by
both backends. Their direction varies by family and parameter pair;
they do not establish an overall SAT speed advantage.
On the 21 initially open
instances, SAT has better upper bounds in \ProgressOpenSATUB{} of
\ProgressOpenPairs{} matched pairs, while ILP has stronger lower bounds
in \ProgressOpenILPLB{} pairs. These findings describe complementary
solution and proof behavior in this cohort, not general equivalence.

Table~\ref{tab:gap-coverage} and Appendix~\ref{sec:proof-closure}
consolidate the final evidence: all 117 intervals are closed with
independent certificates. The disjoint categories are 91 theory/clique,
15 weighted-capacity and 11 replayed UNSAT proofs. The targeted follow-up
closes the 16 remaining intervals and improves ten upper bounds.
These are instance-specific results and do not alter original timings
or solver statuses.

The separate 50-tree $L(3,2)$ study and archived $L(2,1)$ symmetry
experiments provide additional validation with their own protocols.
The symmetry summary in Table~\ref{tab:compact-symmetry} answers RQ4:
CNF reductions depend on the family and need not produce proportional
time reductions. For cycles, the median clause reduction is 12.58\%
while the median time ratio is near one.
Neither finite agreement with reference formulas nor solver agreement
proves a new formula over an entire graph family. Existing SAT and ILP
labeling methods also preclude a first-use claim for these approaches.
The contribution is a documented exact implementation, correctness
arguments and reproducible evidence about its scope and limits.
Separate new-sample construction validation certifies 96 of 207 instances
through the union of five methods. Its family-specific outcomes support
complementary constructions rather than a single dominant heuristic.
The additional matching-bound audit does not change the historical
101/16 closure split or the independent-support count.

A controlled ablation on unseen instances should assess the new bounds
and color-rank-residual encoding separately. GA remains a proposed
upper-bound baseline; no present result establishes superiority or
inferiority relative to it. Complete certification resolves this fixed
cohort, while scalability and general performance remain empirical questions.
