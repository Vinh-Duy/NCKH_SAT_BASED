\section{Problem definition and scope}
\subsection{Definitions and conventions}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a simple undirected graph without self-loops. Define
\[
D_2(G)=\{\{u,v\}\subseteq V:d_G(u,v)=2\}.
\]
The pairs in $D_2(G)$ are unordered and exclude the edges of $G$.
For integers $h\geq k\geq1$, an $L(h,k)$-labeling is a mapping
$f:V\to\mathbb Z_{\geq0}$ satisfying
\begin{align}
|f(u)-f(v)|&\geq h &&\forall\{u,v\}\in E,\\
|f(u)-f(v)|&\geq k &&\forall\{u,v\}\in D_2(G).
\end{align}
The span of $f$ is $\max_v f(v)-\min_v f(v)$, and $\lambda_{h,k}(G)$
is the minimum span. The empty graph is assigned span 0 by convention.
For $h\geq k$, defining the second constraint using distance exactly 2
is equivalent to imposing it on vertices with a common neighbor. The two
conventions must be distinguished when $h<k$ \cite{calamoneri}.

Since the constraints depend only on label differences, translation by
$f'(v)=f(v)-\min_u f(u)$ preserves feasibility and span. Consequently,
the label domain $\{0,\ldots,s\}$ can be used to test whether a labeling
of span at most $s$ exists. There are $s+1$ available labels, but not all
must be used. Vertices at distance greater than 2 may receive the same label.
The graph may be disconnected: there are no constraints between components,
and its optimal span is the maximum of the component optima.

SAT, both ILP formulations, the bounds, the greedy algorithm, and the validator
accept $h,k$, with default values $(2,1)$. The API also supports nonnegative
integer thresholds outside $h\geq k\geq1$, always using distance exactly 2.
The comparison protocol considers $(1,1),(2,1),(3,2)$ on trees, grids,
Erdős--Rényi graphs, and Barabási--Albert graphs. The experimental section
distinguishes validated results from proposed experiments that have not been run.
The main\_r1 tables retain the archived $L(2,1)$ experiments; they are not
results of the general-parameter benchmark.
