\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:submission-results}
\subsection{Cohort, protocol and validation}
The primary experiment fixes 39 graphs and evaluates each with
$(h,k)\in\{(1,1),(2,1),(3,2)\}$, giving 117 graph--parameter tasks. It
includes nine
random labeled trees, 18 Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi (ER) graphs and nine
Barab\'asi--Albert (BA) graphs: each randomized configuration uses
20, 40 and 60 vertices and seeds 0, 1 and 2. ER edge probabilities are
$p=0.15,0.30$; BA adds $m=2$ edges per new vertex. The remaining three
graphs are grids $P_4\square P_5$, $P_5\square P_8$ and
$P_6\square P_{10}$. Disconnected samples are retained; the cohort is
not selected by solver outcome.

CaDiCaL uses order encoding and Gurobi 13.0.3 uses assignment ILP with one
thread and a greedy MIP start. Symmetry breaking is disabled in this
comparison. Three operational repetitions per backend give
\ProgressObservations{} observations. The three parameter pairs define
different configurations, not repetitions. Backends run sequentially in separate processes, with
execution order rotated. Each run has a 30-second external limit;
wall time includes startup, model construction, solving and cleanup.
Final parent-side witness validation is outside this interval.
The manifest records Python 3.13.7, NetworkX 3.6.1 and PySAT 1.9.dev15
on macOS 13.7.8 x86\_64. This comparison uses the same external deadline
for both backends; their individual APIs have different native timeout
mechanisms, which are not treated as equivalent measurements. The
\texttt{isolated-progress-v2} runner retains intermediate feasible
solutions and lower-bound updates at interruption. SAT sends completed
span decisions; Gurobi sends incumbent and bound updates. Interruption
tests check retention of the last accepted snapshot. The archived v1
runner could retain only the initial shared snapshot after an external
timeout; its equal intervals are not evidence of equal solver progress. One Gurobi run
returned \FEAS{} early with an unrecorded native stopping reason;
it remains feasible and is not counted as optimal.

The audit checks all saved labelings against shortest-path constraints,
plus cohort coverage, configurations and interval consistency. The
encoding is also tested against an exhaustive small-instance oracle.
These checks supplement the proof for RQ1; feasibility validation does
not independently verify UNSAT or ILP dual-bound claims. The separate
proof replay in Appendix~\ref{sec:proof-closure} supplies the missing
lower-bound certificates for this cohort. Raw graphs,
labelings and manifests are retained under \path{results/runs/}, with
fingerprinted analyses under \path{results/analysis/}. Historical pilot
and v1 runs are not pooled with v2. Hardware metadata are incomplete,
so the measurements do not specify a portable reference-machine benchmark.
The build target \texttt{make research-package} audits selected evidence,
regenerates tables and illustrations, and compiles both reports. Input and
output hashes prevent silent changes to cached analyses. Reconstructing
historical CNFs requires matching source fingerprints; summarizing their
recorded counts does not reconstruct them. Resuming a benchmark requires
the same configuration, source, environment and schema.

\subsection{Agreement with theory and consolidated evidence (RQ2)}
The equal-parameter tree formula
$\lambda_{h,h}(T)=h\Delta(T)$ and the grid formula
$\lambda_{2,1}(P_n\square P_m)=6$ for $n,m\geq4$ cover 12 instances.
Both backends agree with these values throughout the repeated experiment.
Proofs and applicability conditions appear in the appendix. More generally,
61 instances attain their applicable initial theoretical lower bound;
35 additional instances close through pooled solver evidence.

benchmark below, so these two analyses are not independent graph cohorts.

\subsection{Supplementary genetic-heuristic baseline}
The GA searches vertex permutations and decodes each order by assigning the
smallest label consistent with previously labeled vertices at distance one
and two. Vertices outside these constrained pairs may share labels. We use
OX1 crossover (probability 0.9), segment inversion (0.05), tournament
selection (size 3), elitism and a population of 40. Each of ten algorithmic
seeds receives 1,000 permutation evaluations. A paired random-order
multi-start baseline uses the same decoder and the same number of evaluations;
the graph-insertion-order greedy labeling is included as a one-pass reference.
The tested configuration is fixed, not tuned on a held-out cohort.

All 117 feasible spans are compared with independently certified optima.
Across the cohort, at least one of ten GA runs attains the certified optimum
on 95 instances, versus 85 for random-order multi-start. Comparing the
per-instance median of seed-paired span differences gives 21 GA wins, 96 ties
and no losses; 15 of the 18 ER $L(3,2)$ tasks are GA wins, making that family
the clearest observed difference. These are descriptive outcomes on the
fixed cohort, not an inferential claim of general GA superiority.
