\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\subsection{Scope of the current conclusions}
The formulation and search apply to general simple undirected graphs.
In the primary v2 comparison, Gurobi meets the all-three-OPT criterion
on 91/117 instances, compared with 77/117 for CaDiCaL. Conditional
runtime medians use only the 74 instances meeting this criterion for
both backends, excluding the other 43 from both timing columns.
The lower SAT median on this intersection is not a full-cohort speed
claim. In particular, the direction reverses for ER $p=0.30$ with
$(1,1)$ and $(2,1)$, each represented by only two jointly solved
instances (Table~\ref{tab:family-runtime}). The separate tree screen
extends the size range to 1600 vertices, with 48 of 50 trees attaining
the degree bound. Its short runtimes do not imply that all trees are easy.
Encoding correctness is established independently of these runtime results.

Only cycles use root fixing in the symmetry experiments. Reflections on
product families require explicit automorphisms, not merely equal vertex
degrees. Reducing symmetric solutions does not imply proportional reductions
in clauses or runtime. Families without additional symmetry constraints
must be described as repeated runs of the same model. These results are
not an evaluation of incremental SAT: learned clauses are not reused across spans.

\subsection{Limitations}
The archived confirmation runner retains a shared initial snapshot when an external
timeout precedes the final worker response. The provenance audit in
Section~\ref{sec:optimality-gaps} shows that all FEASIBLE v1 confirmation
observations have this origin. These are valid conservative intervals, but
their equality cannot establish equal native solver progress. A revised
capture protocol now transmits completed SAT span decisions and validated
Gurobi incumbent/dual-bound updates to the parent process. Interruption tests
exercise retention of the last accepted snapshot. The completed v2 experiment in Section~\ref{sec:progress-confirmation}
retains these updates and distinguishes feasible-solution quality from
optimality coverage. Its results do not establish equivalent effectiveness;
the old v1 snapshots remain separate and cannot recover missing progress.

A valid labeling establishes an upper bound. Optimality additionally requires
a matching lower bound, a proof excluding smaller spans, or an exact formula
within its validity domain. Agreement between two backends is a cross-check,
not a general proof. A value between theoretical lower and upper bounds is
consistent with them even if it attains neither. Finite agreement with a
formula is validation evidence, not a proof over an entire family.

Greedy labeling currently supplies incumbents and MIP starts; it has also
been timed as a separate one-pass baseline. The GA adaptation from radio
labeling \cite{sarhan2026genetic} is evaluated with a common permutation
budget, multiple algorithmic seeds and a paired random-order multi-start
control. It reaches a certified optimum on at least one of ten runs for
95/117 tasks, versus 85/117 for random order; the paired-median advantage is
concentrated in ER $L(3,2)$ cases. These outcomes describe this fixed cohort
and one configuration. Feasible GA solutions remain upper bounds unless they
meet a certified lower bound. Its decoder-only timing excludes shared
preprocessing and validation and is not a like-for-like SAT--ILP comparison.

The new screen does not include a direct-versus-order ablation, specialized
tree algorithms, GA parameter sensitivity, or MILP formulations other than assignment. Results for
one formulation/backend cannot establish superiority over all ILP methods.
Graph-generation seeds measure input variation; repeated runs on one graph
measure timing variation. These are not interchangeable. Short runtimes
also reflect startup and licensing overhead; each comparison must use a
consistent timing definition. Although graph structure and constraint
counts may affect runtime, these measurements do not isolate their
effects from formulation, preprocessing and search behavior. Attributing
the ER differences to the number of SAT variables or clauses alone
would require a controlled comparison with phase-specific measurements.

Known formulas and the reference proofs in the appendix validate the
pipeline and are not claimed as new mathematical contributions. The lower
bound $m+4$ for $C_n\circ P_m$ does not establish equality without an upper-bound
construction. Claims about novelty or the complexity of a graph class require
checking the original publications and the precise assumptions of their results.

\subsection{Completed evidence and possible extensions}
The repeated comparison and successive certification studies now establish
all 117 optima. Appendix~\ref{sec:proof-closure} closes the 16 intervals
remaining after the original color-order construction and removes the
remaining reliance on solver-only lower bounds through mathematical
certificates and independently replayed UNSAT traces. This improves
certainty about each instance without changing timed coverage or proving
a formula for a whole graph family.

The constructor was explored after inspecting the unresolved instances.
Applying its fixed configuration to the entire cohort prevents reporting
only its successful attempts, but does not make this a held-out evaluation.
Appendix~\ref{sec:certificate-validation} subsequently evaluates the frozen
constructor and four controls on 69 new graphs. Its 207 parameter instances
are separate from the primary comparison. Insertion order certifies
43/45 dense-ER cases but no sparse-ER cases, and DSATUR with spacing $h$
has greater overall certificate coverage. This supports a limited
structural role, not a universal or speed claim. The matching certificate
in Appendix~\ref{sec:matching-bound} is independently checkable but
improves no bound in either cohort. The unknown native stopping reason of one Gurobi observation also
remains a limitation rather than an inferred explanation.

Further experiments should be separately identified and distinguish
improvements to feasible solutions from improvements to lower bounds or
proof search. Results favoring Gurobi remain part of the comparison.
A subsequent SAT improvement should be evaluated by ablation, for example
solver reuse across spans or more efficient constraint generation. The new independently replayed UNSAT certificates supply the lower-bound
evidence where mathematical certificates are insufficient. Multiple techniques should not be
introduced simultaneously and their combined gains attributed to a single technique.

Further evaluation should distinguish model-construction cost from search
cost and retain instances not solved within budget. This helps identify
where an improvement acts, rather than inferring its mechanism solely
from total runtime or clause counts at one span.
